Definition

What is whole-home smart living

Whole-home smart living means using the home network as a foundation to bring lighting, shading, security, HVAC, audio-visual and appliances onto one control platform, so a space responds as a whole through scenes and automation. It has four layers: the network layer (cabling, Wi-Fi, PoE), the control layer (hubs, gateways, protocols), the execution layer (fixtures, curtains, locks, air conditioning) and the interaction layer (panels, voice, sensors). Judge a system on three questions: does it work offline, does it meet scene response targets, and do the owner hold the devices and the accounts?

Why smart homes are so often regretted#

Most smart home failures are decided long before any device is installed: no neutral wire in the switch box, no network cabling, a service box that cannot fit anything, or a protocol choice that locks you in. Fixing those after the walls are painted means surface conduit, damaged finishes and rework.

SmartHaus has turned 10 years and 3,200+ delivered projects into three reading paths.

1. Knowledge: decide the invisible layers first

Wiring, networking, protocols, budget and construction sequencing set the ceiling of what your smart home can ever be.

2. Tips: make the system feel like a home

Lighting scenes, automation design, elderly care and children's rooms — moving from "it can turn on a light" to "it understands the household".

3. Pitfalls: spend where it matters

Neutral wires, hub selection, curtain power, service box cooling, Wi-Fi roaming and ecosystem lock-in — the six most expensive mistakes, explained.

Three sentences to remember: deciding device locations before construction matters more than choosing brands; get wiring and networking right and you can add devices later; never bet the whole home on a single ecosystem.

Still have questions?

How much does a whole-home smart system cost?

For a 100–140 m² three-bedroom home: entry level (network, basic lighting, curtains, basic security) roughly ¥15,000–30,000; practical level (dimmable lighting, sensors, voice, scene panels) ¥40,000–80,000; premium level (wired dimming, whole-home sensors, HVAC and shading integration) ¥100,000–250,000. The spread comes from lighting circuit count, whether dimming is wired, and panel materials.

Can an existing home be retrofitted?

Yes, with a different approach: prioritise network fixes, use single-live-wire switches or smart bulbs, and choose battery curtain motors or surface trunking to avoid damaging walls. New builds should be treated as a rough-in-first project.

Do I have to stick to one brand?

We advise against it. Keep networking and cabling on open standards (CAT6A, PoE), choose a hub that supports Matter, prefer products with local control and open APIs for lighting and curtains, and mix brands freely for sensors.
