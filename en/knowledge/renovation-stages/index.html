The single most effective move is bringing the smart home team in at the design stage. If they attend the electrical and carpentry handovers, about 90% of rework disappears. Installation is only the last 10% of the work.

Design stage: three mandatory documents#

  1. Point plan — every switch, socket, network point, sensor, camera and AP marked on the floor plan.
  2. Device list — brand, model, quantity, price, and whether Matter or local control is supported.
  3. Scene table — for each scene (arrive, leave, movie, sleep, night, dining): trigger and actions.

A solution without a scene table is not a solution. A device list is just a parts list.

Electrical stage: six sentences for the handover#

  • Neutral wire in every switch box, 60 mm deep boxes.
  • Bigger service box with four sockets on a dedicated breaker.
  • Cable to a ceiling or wall AP position in every room.
  • One socket inside every curtain box, on the motor side.
  • Three network cables at each TV position, labelled.
  • Sockets under kitchen sinks and in bathrooms for water and gas sensors.

Carpentry and ceilings: the two rework hotspots#

  • Curtain box: net width ≥ 200 mm (double track), depth ≥ 150 mm, with an access hatch.
  • Ceiling APs and sensors: confirm cut-out diameter and location before holes are drilled; keep them clear of cove lighting.
  • Leave service access for downlight drivers.

Installation and commissioning: from working to enjoyable#

  1. Register each device with a consistent naming scheme (living-room main light, master-bedroom curtain).
  2. Network tuning: fixed 2.4 GHz channel, isolated IoT SSID, roaming verification.
  3. Test every scene at least three times, including manual override and offline fallback.
  4. Train the household — a separate one-page guide for elderly family members.
  5. Hand over documents: device list, account details, scene table, support contact.

Acceptance criteria (copy into the contract)#

  • Scene response: voice ≤ 1.5 s, panel ≤ 0.5 s.
  • Offline test: lighting, curtains and panels still work without internet.
  • Coverage: ≥ −65 dBm in every room.
  • 72-hour soak: no device offline, no hub reboots.
  • 30 days of free tuning after handover.

Attach these acceptance criteria to the contract. It is worth more than any verbal promise.

Still have questions?

When should the smart home team join the project?

Ideally at the design stage, and never later than the electrical handover. A bolt-on installation at the end almost guarantees missing points and rework.

My builder says they can do smart home too. Should I let them?

Possibly — check three things: do they produce a point plan, can they deliver an acceptance-tested scene table, and who handles after-sales? Missing one of these is a warning sign.

How much does the owner need to learn?

Little, but know three things: how to override an automation manually, how to restart the hub, and how to tell when a device is offline.
