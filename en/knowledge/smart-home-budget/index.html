Budget allocation: three tiers and where the money goes

Budgeting is not about how many devices to buy, but about which items must never be redone. This allocation comes from settled projects.

For a 100–140 m² three-bedroom home: entry ¥15k–30k, practical ¥40k–80k, premium ¥100k–250k. One principle: spend first on what cannot be redone (network, wiring, lighting circuits), then on devices you can add later.

Three tiers (excluding appliances)#

TierTotalCoversBest for
Entry¥15k–30kWhole-home Wi-Fi, basic smart switches, curtain motors, lock, cameras, one hubFirst try, rentals, tight budgets
Practical¥40k–80kAdds dimming, presence sensors, scene panels, voice, basic security, automationOwner-occupied improvements
Premium¥100k–250kWired dimming/partial bus, whole-home sensors, HVAC and shading integration, AV and audioLarge flats, villas, stability-critical

ModuleShareAmountNote

Never cut these#

  1. Network cable and points — small cost now, demolition later.
  2. Neutral wire in switch boxes — retrofitting can cost more than all your switches.
  3. Hub redundancy — one hub failure takes the whole home down.
  4. Commissioning hours — this is what turns hardware into a home.
  5. Curtain power and access hatches — retrofits end up as surface trunking.

Where you can save#

  • Panel materials: aluminium, glass, sintered stone differ 3–8× in price for identical function.
  • Sensor count: start with corridors, bathrooms and the entry, add later.
  • Wall tablets instead of dedicated control screens.
  • AV and multi-room audio can be added after handover.

Reading a quote#

  • Per point or per circuit? Lighting must be quoted per circuit; "per switch" hides circuit counts.
  • Is commissioning included? Require hours and acceptance criteria.
  • Are power and consumables itemised? Sockets, breakers, trunking and back boxes must not become add-ons.
  • Warranty and response times in writing, especially for hubs and network gear.

The three most common add-ons: deeper back boxes and multi-gang panels, extra lighting circuits, and commissioning charged separately. Cap them in the contract.

A real trade-off#

A 120 m² flat originally planned to spend 40% of budget on panels and a control screen. We halved the panel budget, kept two scene screens, and redirected the rest into neutral rewiring, eight network points, three ceiling APs and two redundant hubs. Voice response dropped from 1.5 s to 0.4 s and annual fault tickets fell from six to one.

Still have questions?

Can I do it in phases?

Yes, and we recommend it: rough-in, network and lighting circuits during construction; sensors and automations three to six months after moving in, based on real habits.

Why can quotes for the same flat differ by 3×?

Four variables: wireless vs. wired dimming, lighting circuit count, panel materials, and whether commissioning and after-sales are included. If a quote does not itemise these, it cannot be compared.

Can I buy the devices myself?

Possible if you accept compatibility risk. Responsibility for interoperability sits with you; installers typically guarantee correct wiring only, not working scenes.
