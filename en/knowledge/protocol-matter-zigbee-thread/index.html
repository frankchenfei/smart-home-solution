Protocol selection: Matter, Zigbee, Thread, Wi-Fi or wired

The protocol decides how devices talk. Choose wrong and the devices are not broken — they simply cannot hear each other.

Matter is not a magic protocol — it is an application-layer standard. It standardises how devices are controlled, not how they join a network (that is still Wi-Fi, Thread or Ethernet underneath). The most robust engineering mix: wired backbone (Ethernet/RS485), Zigbee or Thread for sensors and control, Wi-Fi for a few high-bandwidth devices.

What Matter actually solves#

  • Solves: cross-ecosystem control, multi-admin (one device in several ecosystems), standardised local communication.
  • Does not solve: the underlying network (Thread or Wi-Fi); advanced features such as multi-gang panel key customisation, complex lighting effects and curtain calibration often remain vendor-specific; ecosystems differ in how completely they support each device.

Practical view: Matter is an entry ticket, not a feature list. Ask about local control, offline behaviour and feature completeness as well.

Thread and border routers#

  • Keep at least two border routers for redundancy; a single one is a single point of failure for the whole Thread network.
  • Thread relies on stable IPv6 and mDNS — some older routers need settings enabled manually.
  • Thread's device catalogue is still smaller than Zigbee's, especially for sensors.

The Wi-Fi device-count trap#

  • Home routers typically handle 30–50 concurrent 2.4 GHz clients stably.
  • Cheap Wi-Fi devices permanently degrade airtime efficiency and slow your phones down.
  • Rule of thumb: above roughly 40 communicating devices, move lighting, sensors and curtains to Zigbee or Thread.

When wired is worth the money#

  • Large flats, villas, hotel guest rooms and show flats where stability is non-negotiable.
  • Many lighting circuits requiring deterministic dimming (0-10V, DALI, KNX).
  • HVAC, floor heating, fresh air and shading integrated as one system.
  • Cost: roughly 1.5–3× wireless per circuit, with far lower failure rates and a much longer service life.

Recommended mixed strategy#

  1. Backbone: CAT6A plus PoE, wired end to end.
  2. Lighting: Zigbee for the bulk, wired dimming for key areas.
  3. Sensors: Zigbee or Thread, battery-powered first to reduce power points.
  4. Large appliances: Ethernet or local interfaces (RS485, dry contacts); IR only as a fallback.
  5. Hubs: at least two independent paths so no single ecosystem can take you hostage.

Do not compress all lighting, curtains and locks into one vendor's private protocol just because it is cheap. Once locked in, a price rise, an app delisting or a server migration becomes your problem.

Still have questions?

Do Matter devices work without internet?

It depends on the ecosystem. Matter supports local control, so an app or hub on the same LAN should still work when the internet is down, but cloud voice assistants will not. Always ask which functions remain available offline.

Do Zigbee and Thread interfere with each other?

Both use 2.4 GHz, as does Wi-Fi. Fix your 2.4 GHz channel to the cleanest of 1, 6 or 11, and offset Zigbee/Thread to something like 15, 20 or 25.

How many devices can one gateway handle?

Vendor claims are optimistic. In practice, consumer gateways are comfortable with 40–60 devices; beyond that, response times and dropouts increase. Large homes should split by floor or zone and verify cross-gateway scenes.
