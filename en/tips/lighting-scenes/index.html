Lighting scenes: colour temperature, illuminance and dimming

Lighting is the only part of a smart home that is perceived every single day. Get it right and the system earns its keep.

Three rules: one colour-temperature band for the whole home (2700–3500 K), illuminance to standard in key areas (living room 150–300 lx, reading 500 lx, dining 200–300 lx), and dim wherever possible or at least zone it. Lighting done well is what makes scenes meaningful.

Colour temperature#

  • Living room and bedroom: 2700–3000 K (warm, relaxing).
  • Kitchen, bathroom, work areas: 3500–4000 K (neutral, crisp).
  • Never mix two colour temperatures in one sightline.
  • Confirm the tunable range (e.g. 2700–6500 K) and accept that brightness drops slightly at the warmest end.

Illuminance targets#

SpaceTargetNote
Living room (general)150–300 lx50–100 lx for movies
Dining table200–300 lxUse CRI ≥ 90 sources
Kitchen worktop300–500 lxAdd under-cabinet strip
Reading / desk500 lx+Combine with a task lamp
Corridor / entry100–150 lxSensor + low level is nicer
Bathroom mirror300 lx3500–4000 K, avoid backlit shadows

Dimming methods#

The most common dimming failure is a driver/dimmer mismatch: slight flicker, jumps at low level, or a faint glow when off. Test the actual units together before purchase, never the specification sheet alone.

Six essential scenes#

  1. Arrive — entry light on, living room 60%, curtains half open, soft music.
  2. Movie — main lights off, cove 15%, 2700 K, curtains closed.
  3. Dining — pendant 80% with perimeter cove at 30% to light the food.
  4. Sleep — main lights fade over 8–15 s, bedside remains readable, curtains close.
  5. Night — floor or under-bed strip at 5–10% warm, lighting only the route to the bathroom.
  6. Leave — everything off, curtains closed, security armed.

Details that matter#

  • Use aluminium channels with diffusers for LED strips — no visible diodes, no glare.
  • Use mmWave presence sensors, not PIR, so lights do not switch off when someone is sitting still.
  • Keep night lighting below 10% brightness at 2200–2700 K.
  • Entry, corridor and bathroom are the highest-value sensor locations.

Transition times: 0.3–1 s for instant scenes and 8–15 s for sleep or cinema. Making everything 1 s feels abrupt and cheap.

Still have questions?

Do I need a main light at all?

No. The real value of layered lighting design is dimmable, zoneable light — a main fixture plus cove plus spots achieves the same thing. Zoning matters more than whether a central fixture exists.

Can smart bulbs and smart switches coexist?

Yes, but avoid two control paths competing over one fixture. Let the switch control circuit power and the bulb handle dimming and colour, and define clearly in each scene which one is authoritative.

Why does my LED glow faintly when switched off?

Usually leakage current through a shared neutral, driver capacitor discharge or dimmer leakage. Fix it with a driver-matched dimmer, a compensation resistor at the fixture, or a smart switch designed for two-wire operation.
