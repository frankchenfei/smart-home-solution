Elderly care and children: technology aimed at people who need it

The most valuable smart home technology is not the one that impresses visitors — it is the one that makes life easier for people who struggle.

Priority order for elderly care: safety (gas, water, falls) > accessibility (night route lighting, voice) > comfort (climate automation). Always keep physical switches — for an older person, a switch they can feel beats a voice command every time.

Five essentials for elderly residents#

  1. Night route lighting — under-bed strip plus 5% warm bathroom light, illuminating in sequence along the path.
  2. Safety alarms — gas, water and smoke sensors wired to whole-home voice announcements plus phone notifications, not just an app message.
  3. One-touch call — a wireless button by the bed that notifies family phones and announces locally.
  4. Fall and inactivity detection — mmWave radar in bathroom and bedroom (no cameras), notifying if no activity is detected within a set period.
  5. Large-text panels and voice — adequate text height, tactile keys and only three to five voice commands.

Accessibility details#

  • Switch height 0.9–1.1 m for elderly users, to avoid bending.
  • Night lighting at 2200–2700 K so it does not dazzle.
  • Slow down speech responses and keep them short.
  • Let the older person decide which lights should come on automatically — acceptance improves dramatically.

Children's rooms#

  • Study lighting: 500 lx+ at the desk, around 4000 K, CRI ≥ 90, flicker-free.
  • Sleep: fade to dark over 15 s, keep 1% ambient for checks.
  • Study mode: one touch sets desk lamp, main light and quiet mode.
  • Age-appropriate: voice bedtime stories when young; reminders and timers later.

Privacy boundaries: cameras are not the answer#

  • No cameras in bedrooms or bathrooms — use radar and door contacts.
  • Set living room cameras to disable or mask when someone is home.
  • Tell the family exactly what is recorded and where it is stored (local beats cloud).
  • For child monitoring, prefer audio and status notifications over continuous video.

The hard part of elderly care projects is not technology — it is whether the older person will actually use it. The closer a solution is to existing habits, the higher the success rate.

The one-pager you leave with the family#

  1. Three most-used manual actions, and where they are.
  2. What to do first when an alarm fires, and how to clear it.
  3. What still works if the internet goes down.
  4. Who to contact — support number and messenger ID.

Still have questions?

What if my parents refuse to use smart devices?

Start with functions that require no learning: automatic night lighting, gas alarms, automatic lights-off. Zero-effort functions have the highest acceptance.

How accurate is fall detection?

mmWave radar is now reasonably good at identifying prolonged stillness with abnormal posture, but false positives remain. Treat it as an early warning alongside a call button, never as the only mechanism.

Do smart lights harm children's eyes?

The relevant parameters are illuminance, CRI, flicker and glare — not intelligence. Choose flicker-free, CRI ≥ 90, tunable-white products and install to standard illuminance levels.
