Automation: from voice commands to invisible operation

Good automation is like a competent butler: you never notice it, yet it is always there when it matters.

Three principles: predictable (it never acts randomly), overridable (manual action always wins), failsafe (it still works if a sensor dies). With those, ten automations per home is plenty.

Five trigger types#

  1. Time — curtains at 07:00; check for lights left on at 23:30.
  2. Presence — mmWave sensors in corridors, bathrooms and entries.
  3. State — lock unlocked, door opened, device online/offline.
  4. Environment — lux, temperature, humidity, PM2.5, CO₂.
  5. Geofence — phone arriving or leaving (always pair with a fallback time condition).

Structure: trigger → condition → action → fallback#

  • Trigger: what starts it.
  • Condition: whether it should run (only after sunset, only when nobody is home).
  • Action: what happens, with delays and fades allowed.
  • Fallback: what happens on failure (sensor offline → keep state, send notification).

Twelve useful automations#

  1. After the door is deadbolted: lights off, curtains closed, security armed, dehumidifier off.
  2. Arriving after sunset: entry 100%, living room 60%, off after five minutes with no presence.
  3. Bathroom: 30% on entry, off after two minutes idle; 5% warm at night.
  4. Night walk in the master bedroom: under-bed 5%, then the route lights gently.
  5. Movie mode: main lights off, curtains closed, cove at 15%.
  6. Sleep: at 23:30, if the bedroom is occupied and all lights are off, close curtains and set the AC to night mode.
  7. When leaving: if a window is open, notify instead of closing it automatically.
  8. Heat alert: above 30 °C indoors with nobody home, close curtains to slow the rise.
  9. Air quality: CO₂ above 1000 ppm with people present, start fresh air until 800 ppm.
  10. Leak: water sensor triggers a shut-off valve, whole-home announcement and push notification.
  11. Elderly care: no activity detected for 12 hours, notify family.
  12. Weekend mornings: separate wake times, and nothing on public holidays.

Anti-patterns#

  • Time-only triggers — every light on at 07:00 on a Sunday.
  • Sensor-driven lighting with no delay — lights go out as you walk, like a haunted house.
  • Automations fighting each other — A turns off what B just turned on.
  • No manual priority — you switch a light off and it comes back ten seconds later.
  • Ignoring the rest of the household — automations tuned only to the main user's habits.

Every automation must answer two questions: what happens if the sensor fails, and what happens if I contradict it manually? If you cannot answer, do not ship it.

How to roll out#

  1. Log real routines for two weeks.
  2. Ship five core automations and observe for two weeks.
  3. Add one or two at a time so faults stay traceable.
  4. Write one line of documentation per automation for the family.
  5. Review quarterly and delete anything nobody uses.

Still have questions?

Are geofence automations accurate?

Home-grade accuracy is limited and phone power-saving often breaks them. Combine with conditions such as being away for more than ten minutes with no presence indoors, and always keep a manual trigger.

What is the difference between PIR and mmWave presence?

PIR detects movement, so someone sitting still is treated as absent. mmWave presence sensors detect breathing-level micro-motion, which suits studies, bathrooms and bedrooms.

Cloud or local automations?

Prefer local: faster (often under 200 ms), works offline, better privacy. Keep cloud automations for things that need weather or location data.
