Pitfall 3: curtain motor power and box dimensions

Curtains are the most common thing owners wish they had planned for. It takes ten minutes during the electrical and carpentry stages.

Two hard standards: the socket goes inside the top of the curtain box, on the motor side, and a double electric track needs ≥ 200 mm net width and ≥ 150 mm depth. Miss either and you will end up with surface trunking or a single track.

Power provision#

  • Position: inside the top of the curtain box, 100–200 mm below the ceiling, on the motor side, facing the wall.
  • Quantity: one socket for a single track; one or two for a double track.
  • Height: allow the motor lead to hang naturally, never taut.
  • Never place the socket outside the curtain box — the cable will cut across the drapes.
  • Fit a socket even if you defer installation, plus a maintenance access position.

Classic mistake: sizing a manual curtain box (150 mm) for a double electric track — you end up with one track or exposed hardware.

Motor selection#

  • Track (open/close) motors for drapes; check track length and load (sheers add weight).
  • Tubular motors for roller and venetian blinds; check tube diameter and torque.
  • Avoid converted roman poles — compatibility is poor; use a track instead.
  • Manual pull start is essential if elderly family members live there.
  • Confirm manual operation during a power cut.

Non-standard windows#

  • L-shaped and curved windows need bendable or split tracks — confirm during carpentry.
  • Bay windows: check the relationship between sill and box so the motor does not clash.
  • Full-height glazing: recalculate torque and track load; do not reuse a standard specification.
  • Split windows: one motor can drive several panels if the track is unified.

Acceptance checklist#

  • Full travel three times, no noise or binding.
  • Obstruction stop: block the curtain and the motor must stop immediately.
  • Travel limits: fully closed without creasing or gaps.
  • Manual pull start works with power off.
  • Access hatch present in the ceiling.

Curtain motors typically last 5–8 years. Treat replacement as scheduled maintenance and plan the access hatch accordingly.

Still have questions?

Are battery curtain motors worth it?

They suit un-wired older homes and light sheers. You will need to recharge every three to six months, and heavy drapes strain battery units — prefer mains power where possible.

My curtain box is already too small. What now?

Use a slim single track, or move one layer to a roller or venetian blind mounted outside the box. If carpentry is ongoing, widening the box is the best fix.

Are electric curtains noisy?

Quality track motors run at roughly 35–45 dB, similar to a fridge. Loud clicking usually means an unlevel track or a jammed runner.
