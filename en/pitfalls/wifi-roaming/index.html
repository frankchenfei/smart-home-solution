Pitfall 5: roaming stutter and dead zones

Those unexplainable smart home faults — devices offline, slow voice, delayed lights — are almost all network problems.

Three measurable criteria: ≥ −65 dBm in every room, roaming handover under 100 ms, IoT devices on an isolated 2.4 GHz network. If you cannot meet them, do not blame the devices.

Roaming standards: k, v and r#

  • 802.11k shares neighbour information so clients find the right AP faster.
  • 802.11v lets the network suggest a handover.
  • 802.11r is fast BSS transition, reducing authentication delay.

For these to work, APs must be the same brand and series, and the client must support them. Otherwise you get sticky clients — you are in the bedroom while your phone still clings to the living room AP.

AP density and power#

The classic error is maxing out transmit power "for coverage", which keeps far-away clients connected at very low rates with sluggish handovers. Use more points at low to medium power, one AP per two to three rooms, starting around 50–70%.

Dead zones and fixes#

  • Bathroom — masonry and tile; use the corridor AP or a wall unit (damp-rated).
  • Bedside — wall APs blocked by nightstands; raise them or go ceiling-mounted.
  • Kitchen — metal cabinets and hoods; keep APs off the metal path.
  • Balcony or plant platform — add an AP, or convert cameras to wired PoE.
  • Lift lobby and entry — verify lock gateway and doorbell coverage separately.

Three IoT network settings#

  1. Separate SSID, 2.4 GHz only, so legacy devices connect reliably.
  2. Fixed channel (the cleanest of 1/6/11), offset from Zigbee.
  3. Disable "smart connect/auto-optimise" features that silently change channels and drop devices.

How to accept the work#

  • Per-room speed and RSSI recorded as a coverage map.
  • Walk-and-talk voice test from the door to the master bedroom.
  • Offline test with the WAN disconnected.
  • 24-hour online-rate review, with each dropout investigated.

If your provider only shows a speed test from the living room, ask for a room-by-room walkthrough. Complaints almost always come from the rooms that were not tested.

Still have questions?

Mesh or AC+AP?

If you can run cable and have many devices, choose AC+AP. If the home is finished, choose Mesh with wired backhaul where possible. The difference is roaming control and multi-AP management, not novelty.

Why do my smart devices keep dropping off?

Three usual causes: 2.4 GHz congestion on the router, excessive distance to the gateway, and auto-channel features that force reconnection. Isolate IoT on its own fixed-channel network.

How much area should one AP cover?

In brick-and-mortar homes estimate 60–90 m² per AP depending on walls, or up to about 120 m² in open plan. Never rely on one high-power AP for a whole home.
