Pitfall 4: the service box is too small, too hot and has no sockets

The service box is your home's data centre. Once it is too small, sealed and crammed, every network problem begins there.

Minimum specification: at least 500×400×120 mm, four sockets inside on a dedicated breaker, metal enclosure with vents, accessible for maintenance. Overheating and too few sockets cause more dropouts than any device fault.

Size and contents#

Developer-standard boxes are often only 300×200 mm — enough for an ONT and nothing else. A real home network needs room for a router, an 8–16 port PoE switch, hub power supplies and a NAS brick.

Recommended: 500×400×120 mm or larger, or replace it with a 6U wall-mounted mini cabinet.

Power#

  • At least four sockets inside, or a 4–6 way switched strip.
  • Its own breaker, so you can isolate everything for maintenance.
  • A small UPS (500 VA is plenty) prevents transient outages from rebooting devices and losing configuration.

Cooling and noise#

  • Metal enclosure with vents top and sides; sealed plastic boxes cook their contents.
  • Move PoE switches and NAS units into a small cabinet outside the box.
  • Above roughly 45 °C internal temperature, failure rates rise sharply. A thermostat-controlled fan helps — keep it under 25 dB.
  • Never bury the box inside a fully closed wardrobe or shoe cabinet.

Location and access#

  • Corridors, entries and utility areas near the incoming supply.
  • The door must open fully with at least 800 mm of working space in front.
  • If it must sit inside cabinetry, fit louvred doors or leave ventilation gaps.

Cable management#

  • Label both ends of every cable (room + purpose + number) and stick a point plan on the inside of the door.
  • Use short patch leads at the switch.
  • Reserve two or three spare conduits to the ceiling for future APs or cameras.
  • Photograph everything before the walls close.

Sealing the service box inside a ceiling or cupboard is one of the most expensive oversights in home networking. Diagnosing a fault may mean opening the ceiling again.

When to move up to a mini cabinet#

Choose a 6U–9U wall cabinet if any two of these apply: 16+ network points; a NAS, NVR or PoE cameras; AV or distributed audio needing central terminations; a villa or duplex with devices on several floors.

Still have questions?

Can the developer's service box be enlarged?

Yes, by cutting in a larger enclosure, though the wall finish must be made good and load-bearing walls should not be chased extensively. Alternatively, keep the original box as a pass-through and move devices into a nearby cabinet.

Can the ONT sit inside the box?

Yes, but remember a metal box shields Wi-Fi — disable the ONT/router Wi-Fi and cover the home from external APs.

How do I size a PoE switch?

Total PoE budget with 30% headroom (ceiling APs 8–15 W, cameras 5–12 W) and confirm the per-port standard (802.3af/at/bt) matches your devices.
