Three moves: bring smart home forward to the electrical handover, price in three tiers, and outsource commissioning and after-sales to specialists so it never becomes a service black hole.

Why most renovation companies struggle#

  • Smart home is scheduled last, after points are fixed.
  • Quotes list device counts, so customers only see "a pile of switches".
  • An electrician wires it up, nobody commissions it, and handover becomes the first complaint.
  • Responsibility blurs between device faults, network faults and installation faults.

Each package comes with a point plan template, installation standard, acceptance checklist, customer handbook and a clear after-sales boundary document.

Workflow with clear ownership#

  1. Design: your designer produces the layout; we produce the point plan and scene table.
  2. Electrical handover: we attend and brief the electrician.
  3. Electrical acceptance: spot checks, photos, signed confirmation sheet.
  4. Installation and commissioning: we install and commission; you handle making good and cleaning.
  5. Customer training: 30 minutes on site plus a one-page guide.
  6. After-sales: device and system issues are ours (24-hour response); building work stays with you.

Pricing that survives negotiation#

  • Quote by space and scene, not by device count — a three-bedroom pack of four scenes (arrive, leave, movie, sleep).
  • Give an anchor: compare with appliances or soft furnishings at the same price point.
  • Put acceptance criteria in the quote; it is visible proof of professionalism.
  • Offer phased delivery: rough-in, network and lighting during construction; sensors afterwards.

What you gain#

  • Average contract value up 8–20%.
  • Higher referral rates, because smart living is a visible experience.
  • Fewer disputes thanks to early point confirmation.
  • Predictable after-sales cost, carried by a specialist team.

Pilot on three to five show flats first. Turn the real fault list and customer feedback into sales scripts before rolling out.

Still have questions?

Do we need dedicated technical staff?

No. One project manager as the interface is enough; we handle implementation and commissioning and provide installation standards and training.

What if a customer changes their mind?

The rough-in items are a small share of cost and cause no harm to a conventional fit-out. Keep them and sell them later as upgrade readiness.

How is margin shared?

Two common models: a fixed margin for the renovation company with us carrying delivery cost, or a project gross-margin split. It depends on scale and territory.
